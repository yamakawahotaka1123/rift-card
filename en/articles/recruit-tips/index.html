How to write a LoL friend or duo post that gets replies

Friend and duo posts on X and in Discord looking-for-group channels move fast. To make someone think “I want to play with this person,” the shortest path is to put what readers want to know where they can see it at a glance. Here is how to write a post that gets replies.

What readers check first

Most people decide whether you are a good match in roughly this order:

  1. Rank: can you queue together, and is your level similar?
  2. Role: do your roles clash? (This matters most for bot lane pairs.)
  3. Play style: tryhard ranked, or relaxed normals and ARAM?
  4. Play times: do your hours overlap?
  5. Voice chat: do you want to talk, or text only?

If these five come across in the first few lines (or in an image), people can reply or DM you without hesitating. A post that only says “anyone welcome!” is harder to answer, because nobody can picture who you are.

Keep the text short and put details in an image

Posts on X have a character limit, and long text often gets cut off or skipped. That is where a summoner card helps. Put your rank, roles, best champions, play times and tags on one image, and the post itself only needs a greeting and the one thing you most want to say.

Images stand out in the timeline, and a row of champion icons and a rank emblem tells people “this is a LoL post” in an instant. SUMMONER CARD makes a card like this from a few choices.

Example posts you can copy

Looking for a ranked duo

ADC main in Gold, looking for a support to climb to Platinum together! Usually on weeknights after 10 pm. Voice or no voice, either is fine. Details in the image

Looking for casual friends

I play normals and ARAM at a relaxed pace. Would love people who can laugh off mistakes! New players welcome, happy to share what I know. Usually on weekend evenings

Building a team of five (Clash and similar)

Looking for a TOP and a JG for Clash. Silver to Gold, able to practice about once a week. We use Discord voice

Each example makes it clear who you want. Narrowing it down usually brings more replies from people who actually fit.

What to avoid

  • Only listing rules: “no flamers, no trolls, no one below X” sounds harsh. Try saying it positively, like “I’d love to play with people who keep it fun.”
  • Inflating your rank: it gets awkward once you play together. Your honest current rank and goal attract people with the same goal.
  • Sharing too much personal info: leave out your real name, school and where you live. See Using your summoner card safely on social media.

After someone replies

Thank them and explain how to add you in game. If your Riot ID (Name#Tag) is not on the card, sending it by DM keeps things smooth. For the first game or two, focus on whether playing together is fun rather than on winning.

If it does not click, you do not have to keep going. A quick “Thanks for the games, maybe next time!” is enough, and you can post again.

Summary

  • Lead with rank, role, play style, play times and voice chat
  • Keep the text short and put the details on your summoner card
  • Say who you want to play with, rather than listing what you don’t want
